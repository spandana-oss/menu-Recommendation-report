01 · About This Report

How These Recommendations Were Built

This is a new-menu development exercise, not a refresh of an existing menu — all seven items below are new introductions, generated for a restaurant that does not yet have a menu in this ZIP. Every claim in this report is labeled by type, so you always know whether you're looking at a fact, a calculation, or a judgment call.

D

Real Data

U.S. Census-style demographic, income, and commuter-flow figures for ZIP 38114, plus the actual list of 19 real restaurants operating in this ZIP.

C

Calculated Values

Numbers derived directly from the real data using a stated formula — household income-bracket shares, gross profit, food cost %, and profitability %.

I

Business Interpretation

Judgment calls that translate data into strategy — for example, reading "no cuisine-type field" restaurant names to infer a local style, or setting a price band.

E

Reasoned Estimates

Directional estimates built from real inputs where no single measured field exists — such as splitting a weekday vs. weekend customer mix using commuter data.

What data was used

ZIP-level population, income brackets, age, household size, employment and labor-force data; commuter inflow/outflow (who lives here vs. who works here); restaurant count and density; and the real names of the 19 existing restaurants in ZIP 38114.

What data was NOT used

This information is not available in the provided dataset and has not been estimated: customer reviews, star ratings, POS sales history, nutrition/calorie data, and a direct cuisine-type field for the comparable restaurants (their style is inferred from restaurant names only).

!

Comparable restaurants in this ZIP are used only to validate that a food style is already viable here — they are never the source of a recommendation. 05 · Profitability Analysis

The Only Formulas Used in This Report

Profitability is the one place in this report driven by fixed formulas. Every other judgment call — cuisine fit, segment fit, confidence score — is a qualitative business interpretation, not a calculation. Worked example below uses Bourbon Smoke Tenders.

Gross Profit = Selling Price − Theoretical Cost
Food Cost % = ( Theoretical Cost ÷ Selling Price ) × 100
Profitability % = ( Gross Profit ÷ Selling Price ) × 100
Theoretical Cost = Ingredient Cost + Prep Cost
3-Month Profit = Gross Profit × 1.9
6-Month Profit = 3-Month Profit × 1.9
9-Month Profit = 6-Month Profit × 1.9
Ingredient Cost
The raw cost of the food items that go into one serving (e.g., chicken, breading, glaze).
Prep Cost
The added cost of labor, cooking, and handling to turn raw ingredients into the finished dish.
Theoretical Cost
Ingredient Cost + Prep Cost — what the dish should cost to make under normal conditions.
Selling Price
The recommended menu price charged to the customer.
Gross Profit
What's left from the selling price after Theoretical Cost is covered.
Food Cost %
The share of the selling price consumed by making the dish. Lower is better.
Profitability %
The share of the selling price kept as profit. Always equals 100% − Food Cost %.
3 / 6 / 9-Month Profit
Comparing all 7 recommended items shows the same pattern every time: each period's figure is the prior period's profit multiplied by a consistent 1.9× growth factor, starting from the item's own Gross Profit. This ~1.9× per-quarter step was reverse-engineered by checking the ratio across all 7 items — it likely represents an assumed order-volume ramp — and holds exactly for every item in the source file.
What is the 1.9×?
In plain terms: for every $1 of profit a dish makes today,the model is assuming 90% quarter-over-quarter growth. It is a volume-growth assumption baked into the source file, not something this report invented or calculated independently.
